A good examination does more than give a score. It shows each student exactly what to work on next.

Every part of this examination was designed personally, and every paper will be assessed the same way. Each question is here to show what a student can use independently, not simply what they can recognise.

Twenty-five years of teaching sit behind these thirty questions — university lecture halls, examination classes, and a great many hours spent working out why a capable student still loses marks. That is what the paper is built to find.

The grammar section tests four tenses the class has studied properly. The vocabulary section asks for meaning in context rather than translation. The writing task is long on purpose — three hundred words should start to feel ordinary rather than frightening.

Results are not shown on screen. The written work is read by the teacher first, so that each candidate receives one considered assessment rather than a number followed later by an opinion.
